# Project 4 – Track 1: Smarter Campus
### ML for Occupancy & Space Usage

## 1. Project Overview

A university campus is a complex **Cyber-Physical System**, where hundreds of people move every day between classrooms, laboratories, study areas, and common spaces. These activities generate flows, occupancy patterns, and operational needs that often remain largely unobserved.

How can we identify which classrooms are actually available? How can we determine whether a route is accessible to people with mobility impairments? How does occupancy vary throughout the day? Who is authorized to access a laboratory, and at what times?

Answering these questions requires instrumenting the physical environment and transforming heterogeneous sensor signals into meaningful information that can support real-world decisions.

The **Smarter Campus** project explores how sensing technologies, embedded systems, and Machine Learning can be combined to develop innovative solutions addressing real needs within the university environment.

## 2. The Arduino UNO Q at the Core

The central hardware platform of this project is the **Arduino UNO Q**, a hybrid computing board that combines a Linux-capable microprocessor with a dedicated microcontroller for real-time sensor and actuator management.

This architecture enables the development of solutions that combine:

- **Sensing and Data Acquisition:** collecting information from the physical environment through sensors and connected devices.
- **Edge Computing:** processing data directly on the board, reducing latency and dependence on external infrastructure.
- **Machine Learning:** applying models for classification, prediction, occupancy estimation, and anomaly detection.
- **Cloud Integration:** connecting local devices to external services when remote processing, storage, or interoperability is required.
- **Actuation and Automation:** translating insights into actions through connected devices and actuators.

A key aspect is understanding when processing should take place locally and when cloud services are actually needed. Edge-based solutions can reduce communication overhead and limit the transmission of potentially sensitive information, particularly when dealing with people's presence and movements.

## 3. Learning Objectives

The lectures are designed to provide the **background knowledge and technical foundations** needed to explore the possibilities of smart campus applications.

Topics include:

- Cyber-Physical Systems and IoT architectures.
- Occupancy monitoring and space utilization.
- Machine Learning for classification and prediction.
- Forecasting and anomaly detection.
- Edge sensing and on-device inference.
- Hardware/software integration and deployment.
- Edge versus cloud architectures.

These topics represent a toolbox rather than a mandatory checklist: **not every technology or concept covered during the lectures needs to be integrated into the final project.**

The goal is to understand the available technologies and select only those that are relevant to the problem being addressed.

## 4. Project Development Guidelines

The objective is not simply to build a prototype that combines sensors, Machine Learning, and connected devices. Each project should start from a clearly identified problem and develop a meaningful, feasible solution.

Every proposed solution should:

1. **Address a real need:** identify a concrete problem or opportunity within the university campus.
2. **Define a clear objective:** specify what the prototype aims to achieve and what problem it intends to solve.
3. **Identify a target user:** define who will benefit from the solution (e.g., students, university staff, facility managers, laboratory technicians, or people with disabilities).
4. **Be technically feasible:** develop a working prototype based on realistic hardware, software, and data requirements, leveraging the Arduino UNO Q as the core platform.
5. **Be scalable:** consider how the solution could evolve beyond the initial prototype and be deployed in other rooms, buildings, or university campuses.

The choice of sensors, Machine Learning techniques, processing architecture, and external services should follow the project's objectives and requirements, rather than being included simply for the sake of using a particular technology.

## 5. Possible Application Areas

Potential directions for exploration include:

- **Smart Space Management:** classroom availability, occupancy estimation, and study-space utilization.
- **Accessibility:** monitoring environmental conditions and identifying accessible routes and facilities.
- **Smart Access Control:** managing laboratory access through digital permissions, identity verification, and time-based authorization.
- **Environmental Intelligence:** monitoring indoor conditions and identifying anomalies that may affect comfort or space utilization.
- **Campus Automation:** connecting sensing systems to services and actuators to support smarter facility management.

These are examples to inspire project ideas, not predefined solutions or mandatory project requirements.
